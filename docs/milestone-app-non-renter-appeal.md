# Milestone App — Why Use It If You Never Book a Room?

**Problem:** Most of the app's value today is tied to the building: book a studio, show up, create. For someone who isn't renting yet, or who will never physically come to Verdun, there is very little reason to open the app a second time.

**Goal:** Make the app useful on its own, with nothing required on site. The building should feed the app (content, gear, jobs, expertise), and the app should deliver that to people anywhere. Renting a room then becomes one way to use Milestone, not the only one.

---

## 1. Who the non-renter actually is

| Segment | What they want | Why they're not booking |
|---|---|---|
| **Remote creatives**: producers, beatmakers, editors, designers, artists outside Montreal | Get paid, get heard, get better | Not in Montreal, or they already have a home setup |
| **Emerging artists, not ready yet** | Learn, protect their music, build a release plan | Can't justify the cost yet |
| **Clients/buyers**: brands, labels, artists who need a service | Hire reliable talent quickly | They want the *output*, not the room |
| **Community/fans**: followers of JVMES GRAY and Source Pop | See inside, be part of it | Curious, not yet creating |

Each segment needs its own reason to open the app. One "book a studio" home screen serves none of them.

---

## 2. Value pillars that don't require being on site

### A. Get work → the marketplace as an income source
The app is already designed as a marketplace where creatives build profiles and clients hire them directly. Make that the main reason remote creatives join:
- **Remote-deliverable gigs first:** mixing, beats, toplines, video editing, colour, thumbnails, cover art, motion graphics, voiceover, translation (FR/EN). These never need the building.
- **Agency overflow → app briefs.** When Milestone's creative agency or video department lands more work than in-house can handle, post the overflow as paid briefs that only app members can claim. This makes Milestone a source of real jobs, not just a listing site.
- **Trust layer:** Milestone-verified profiles, held payments (Stripe), and standard contracts generated by the app. Clients pay a premium for "vetted by Milestone." Creatives get paid on time.

### B. Protect your rights → the free utility every indie artist needs
The IP & royalties AI agent is the strongest standalone hook, and almost nobody offers it in French:
- Split-sheet generator plus e-signature for every collaboration
- Release checklist: SOCAN, ISRC, distribution, neighbouring rights (Re:Sound/ARTISTI), metadata
- "Is this contract fair?" quick review for label, producer, and sync deals (education, not legal advice, with a referral path)
- Royalty registration tracker: "you released 3 songs; 2 aren't registered with SOCAN"

Make this **free**. It's the reason an artist in Sherbrooke, Toronto, or Paris downloads the app, and it builds the habit and trust that lead to paid services.

### C. Use the room without being in the room → remote services
Turn the gear itself into a product:
- **Remote mix/master through the SSL 9000J:** upload stems, choose an engineer, get an analog mix back, and approve revisions in-app. Price it per song.
- **Remote-attended sessions:** stream a live session to an artist so they can give notes in real time from anywhere.
- **Podcast/video post-production as a service:** send raw files and get an edited episode or reel back.

This turns the building's capacity into revenue from people who will never walk in.

### D. Get better → Milestone Academy online
- Short lessons filmed in the actual rooms (the console, the Content Creation Room, the podcast studio)
- Session breakdowns: "how this track was made," including JVMES GRAY / Source Pop material
- **Feedback queue:** submit a track or reel and get timed written or voice notes from a Milestone engineer or A&R (paid, or included in a tier)
- Certificates that appear on the creative's marketplace profile, so learning helps them get hired (A)

### E. Belong → community and opportunities
- **Challenges:** remix contests using released stems, 48-hour edit challenges, cover-art briefs. Winners get studio time, a feature, or a paid placement.
- **Collab board:** "need a vocalist / need a beat / need an editor," matched by profile
- **Live drops:** monthly streamed listening sessions or masterclasses from the building
- **Behind-the-scenes feed:** what's being made at Milestone this week (with the creatives' consent)

## 3. Make it visible on first open

Replace the generic onboarding with one question:

> **What are you here to do?**
> Get hired · Hire someone · Protect my music · Learn · Book the studio

Each answer leads to a different home screen. "Book the studio" becomes one option of five, so non-renters never land on a screen that isn't meant for them.

---

## 4. Membership shape (hypothesis to test)

| Tier | For | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | Everyone | Profile, rights toolkit (split sheets, release checklist), community, browse briefs |
| **Remote member** | Working remote creatives | Claim paid briefs, verified badge, monthly feedback credit, full Academy library, member rates on remote mix/master |
| **Studio member** | People who come in | Everything above plus booking, hours, and on-site perks |

Price points are deliberately left blank. Test them with real users before committing.

---

## 5. What to build first

Ranked by (uses what already exists) × (value without the building) × (speed):

1. **Rights & royalties toolkit (free).** The AI agent already exists; wrap it as split-sheet + release-checklist flows. This is the acquisition hook.
2. **Remote mix/master + post-production storefront.** Turns existing gear and staff into revenue from anywhere, with a simple upload → pay → deliver → revise flow.
3. **Paid briefs feed seeded with agency overflow.** This is the retention loop: remote creatives come back because there is work.
4. Onboarding fork ("What are you here to do?")
5. Academy online plus the feedback queue
6. Challenges and the collab board

All of it ships FR/EN, with French as the default (Bill 96).

---

## 6. How we'll know it's working

- **% of weekly active users who have never booked a room.** This is the main metric.
- **% of revenue from users who have never booked.**
- Time from sign-up to first paid gig (for creatives)
- Rights toolkit: split sheets created per week; releases with complete registration
- Conversion from remote member to first in-person booking (the building still benefits)
